Not long ago, I decided to build my genealogy tree. I had started it once when I was a teen, but I didn't put that much effort into it. It probably stopped around my great-grandparents' generation and that was it. But this time I decided to dig deeper!

<!--more-->

![A Baptism From 1907, Santana da Serra](image:2026-11-02-family-tree-1)
{width="1121" height="1500" class="right"}

As many of you know, I'm Portuguese and I've been living in the Netherlands for over 6 years. And, as far as I know, my family's history doesn't cover anything interesting: everyone is Portuguese, comes from Portugal, and that's about it. Nevertheless, I've always wanted to dig further into past generations and see what it looks like.

Since 1911, the Portuguese government keeps official records for births, deaths, and other life events. Those can be requested by virtually anyone, as long as you pay for them.

The trick here is to ask for the birth certificate, since it includes "amendments" (_averbamentos_) which include all the necessary information about marriages, deaths, and other life events. It also includes the parents' and grandparents' names, as well as the parents' ages when the child was born.

I think the format is based on the church's record formats. The church kept baptism, death and marriage records before 1911. Luckily, these are almost all scanned and available online through DigitArq, from the district archives in Beja and Faro and from Torre do Tombo.

They're organized by parish, type and year, although sometimes one parish's books are split between archives. The trick is finding the right people inside.

![Baptisms From Around 1817, Santana da Serra](image:2026-11-02-family-tree-2)
{width="5406" height="4350"}

Some of the older records are a bit hard to decipher. The good thing is that they're almost always based on a fixed formula. You know what to expect, the sentences are the same, you just need to fill in the blanks and the names. Claude has turned out to be quite good at deciphering text, especially when it figures out the formula.

Sometimes it is hard to find the records, for several reasons. The age of the married couple doesn't always match the real age, so I need to look for a baptism around the target year, but I've seen as much as 2 years of difference.

In some years there are missing records, and later "aberturas", where these records have been back-filled based on memory, and potentially wrong.

And, the further back you go, the less information you get. From a certain point on, marriage records stop recording the age of the couple on the day, and baptism records stop recording the actual birth date.

Then there are the names. Sometimes the father is "unknown", sometimes the name is not complete, or the person got a second first name during their adult life.

For example, you could be baptized as Maria, and later in life be known as Maria Antónia. But working back from the present to the past makes it harder to match which Maria is the real Maria Antónia, especially when a couple has multiple daughters called Maria. Sometimes it is possible, sometimes it isn't.

Last names also weren't always used or recorded consistently, which means that sometimes I don't see those in older records. Most times the wife ends up getting the feminized version of the husband's last name, sometimes she doesn't even get a last name, at least not on the records.

I've been using [MacFamilyTree](https://www.syniumsoftware.com/macfamilytree) to keep my family tree. I decided to go for a desktop software for the reliability and customizability. I couldn't find any online website that allowed me to be as thorough as with MacFamilyTree.

![Part of My Family in MacFamilyTree](image:2026-11-02-family-tree-3)
{width="2820" height="1808"}

The bonus is that it also syncs with [FamilySearch](https://www.familysearch.org), but only the information I want to share. I use FamilySearch to publish the researched information, and, since it is a public tree, maybe other people will find themselves there. I only sync people that are no longer alive, for privacy reasons, of course.

Also, FamilySearch has lots of Portuguese records indexed. But they also come with issues: sometimes names are wrong, or make no sense at all. I think they were probably automatically transcribed or something, which ended up with a lot of malformed records. Unfortunately, I haven't found a way to correct those records.

Until now, I've uncovered around 707 people across 7 parishes in Alentejo and Algarve. I've got as far back as the early to mid 1700s in some branches, and the search hasn't ended yet. Ideally, I'd go as far back as the online records provide, but the further back I go, the harder it becomes. Fewer ages, fewer dates, less clear inferences, sometimes damaged records, etc.

Just like I said in the beginning, I hadn't expected my family to have roots in some other country, and it doesn't, but it's quite fascinating to see that over 8 generations have stayed within the same set of 7 parishes. It's also been very interesting to see how the name choices vary over the years.

Progress has slowed down over the past few weeks, and I don't think I'll focus on it as actively as I did before, but, once in a while, I'll keep working on expanding the tree!
