I'm looking for a new role. Despite having mentioned it in a few blog posts over the past month, and adding a little ["Open to Work" notice](/2026/10/13/redesign/) in my website's header, I haven't really talked much about it here. Until now.

<!--more-->

Since the end of the summer, I've known that there was a chance that the company I work at wouldn't continue as-is from next year. I got that confirmation at the end of September, and since then I've been looking for a new job. In retrospect, I could've started earlier.

## Learning Process

If you look at my [resume](/resume/), you'll notice that my last two positions were shorter than the one before. The good thing is: now I know what I want. And that's also why I want to write this blog post. I want to verbalize -- or wordize? -- my thought process, my previous experiences, what I learned, and what changed.

Everything started almost 10 years ago now. I [joined Protocol Labs](/2018/10/27/working-at-protocol-labs/) as a fresh student at university. I stayed there for almost 7 years. I worked [fully remotely](/2023/10/16/after-working-remotely-full-time-one-year/), on exciting open-source projects, asynchronously, and with people from all over the world.

I learned so much, and my work varied a lot over the years. It started with just an Electron app and UI, and gradually shifted into Go services that ended up serving the public gateways, with some stops here and there for specifications and other projects. It was incredible and I'm still very grateful. Unfortunately, the journey ended a year and a half after I finished my master's degree.

At that point, it had only been a few years since I had [moved to a foreign country](/2020/03/30/a-new-adventure/), during Covid-19, and I had had very little social contact for quite some time. At uni, I only had one course on campus, and when things were easing, it was time for my [master's thesis](/2022/09/07/thesis-blocklearning-framework/). And at work, because of Covid-19 and because I was working part-time, I had barely seen anyone *physically* in a long while.

That had a big impact on my next choice: an on-site job. I did not want to have to commute far, so I managed to find something on the street where I live. It was a completely different job, a very small team, in a field that I was interested in: digital credentials. Sadly, after a year, I decided to leave for a few reasons, one being that I really wanted to work at a place where open source was more appreciated and used. I did not want to reinvent the wheel.

At that time, I found Animo Solutions, which worked in the same space, but with a much larger open-source focus. While the platform is closed-source, all of the underlying libraries that actually do the work, implement the specifications, and so forth, are open-source, and under the [OpenWallet Foundation](https://openwallet.foundation/). It's been a joy, from the people I met, to the work we do, to the projects and pilots we did.

I've been working on and building so many features end to end, from reading the specifications and implementing the protocols, to designing how the full flow should work, the backend, the API and the UI, to writing changelogs and docs. This is definitely something I really enjoy. I don't want to be fully confined to a single component, or, for example, to just the backend, without ever having the opportunity to touch other areas. I like being able to see the whole process.

On the side, I've also been maintaining open-source projects for almost a decade now. It's crazy to me that File Browser recently reached 10 years, more than 35K stars and over 100M downloads. While I stand behind [archiving the project](/2026/07/28/filebrowser/), it's still something I'm proud of.

## What Am I Looking For?

And now is the time to look for something new. After almost 10 years -- not necessarily full-time -- working at different companies, I'm a bit more aware of what I want. As they say: you live and you learn.

In terms of work "mode", I think remote and async fits me the best, as long as I'm working in a team with a few people in a company with a few dozen people at least. I have [realized](/2024/07/09/thoughts-after-one-month-working-on-site/) that I do not need -- or want -- to see my coworkers in person every single day. I also don't need to see them necessarily every single week or month. As long as we meet regularly, maybe once a quarter or once every six months, I will be happy enough. This depends, of course, on whether it's a completely remote role or hybrid.

In terms of the work itself, I'd rather work on something where developers are the customers, so most likely something in the realm of developer tooling. And in a company where open source is paramount or where the main product is actually open source. An exception to this would be working on a product I'd actually use.

The past two and a half years have been focused on digital identity and on specifications that were being finalized for legislation that is yet to come. This is to say that it was super specialized work in an emerging area. While a lot of the work and learnings can be extrapolated to other areas, I notice that it is sometimes hard to explain *how*. Implementing specifications, X.509 certificates, PKI, OAuth, interoperability and conformance testing, owning features end to end. These are all things I've done, and none of them are specific to digital identity.

When it comes to technology, I've been mostly working with TypeScript in my last two roles, and before that it was mostly Go with some TS on the side. I don't really have a preference that would block me from choosing a company, but secretly I'd like to work with Go again. I still use it in almost all my side projects where it makes sense, but that's not *a lot*. I am, of course, open to learning and using other things, and I have changed stacks before.

---

I know my "wants" exclude a lot of companies, but they are also ideals. Just like no one fits perfectly into a job description, no company will perfectly fit what I'm looking for. So I will stay flexible, while aiming for these ideals. I still think it's better to try and find something that I appreciate long-term than to settle for something that I will leave within one year.

In short, I'm looking for a software engineer role: Go or TypeScript (others are possible too, I just need to learn them!), backend-leaning, working end to end, and remote or hybrid in the Netherlands, depending on what the commute would look like from Eindhoven. If you know some company that you think would be great for me, [let me know](/more/#contact). I'm available from January, or earlier if needed. Wish me luck!
