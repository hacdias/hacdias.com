Hi everyone!

I've been living in the Netherlands for over 6 months now as a student and I've been observing some things in the healthcare system and I'm genuinely curious on how and why it works like it does in some aspects.

I come from a different country in the EU where we have a mixed public and private healthcare. However, there is a central system where all hospitals - no matter whether they are public or private - can access your data and add information about the patient. As far as I remember, the patient needs to give permission to the doctor (if it's a new doctor) for them to be able to access old information.

Since it's relatively new and there were many systems in the past in different regions, not all the information is available. But all my vaccinations since I was born, scheduled vaccinations for the future and medication receipts, hospital visits and some tests results are available on the platform. Usually private hospitals don't add the information, but you can add it for example.

With the developments of the Covid-19 vaccine in the Netherlands I've been quite confused about how it works here. It seems there was never a place to register people's vaccinations and that not even now all vaccinations are being recorded. Thus, we have a number composed by recorded vaccinations + estimated ones.

My question is: why does it work like that? Why was there never a place to store such information? Who stores such information? How can I access my medical record? What happens if you need to go to a hospital in an emergency and you have some allergy that is not registered anywhere and you're unconscious? Is it related to data privacy? If so, why can't there just be a permission system?

I know, the last question was highly specific, but it's just an hypothetical situation.

Thanks in advance for everyone that replies :)
